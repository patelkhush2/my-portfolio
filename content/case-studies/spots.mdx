Spots is a travel discovery and planning product that connects the places people find with the trips they want to take. I’m the sole product designer, working with a small NYC-based startup team alongside the CEO, Product Managers, and Engineers. My work spans the complete experience, from discovery and creator recommendations to AI-assisted planning and interactive itineraries.

## 01 / The Opportunity

### Finding Places Is Easy. Making Plans Is Harder.

Travel inspiration can come from a friend's restaurant recommendation, a creator's hotel suggestion, or a collection of saved places. But those recommendations rarely add up to a complete trip. People still need to bring them together, decide what fits, and organize them around their time and preferences. The opportunity behind Spots was to connect discovery with planning: what if the places you discover could become the starting point for your next trip?

## 02 / Defining the Product

### Three Experiences. One Connected System.

I structured Spots around three activities: Discover, Plan, and Organize. Discover helps people find places through recommendations, categories, lists, and creator profiles. Plan lets them describe a trip, bring in saved places or reference materials, and explore possible itineraries. Organize gives them a day-by-day schedule to review and arrange activities.

Each activity needed its own interface while staying connected to the others. The main architectural challenge was deciding which features belonged together, how people would move between them, and what information needed to carry from discovery into planning.

## 03 / Designing the Experience

### Decision 01 — Make Discovery Useful Beyond Saving.

I designed discovery interfaces, category browsing, place details, curated lists, and creator profiles to help people find places worth visiting. Images, descriptions, related places, and the person sharing a recommendation give people more context when deciding what to keep. I wanted that context to remain useful after saving, so I designed lists and recommendations as inputs to the planning experience. The goal was to connect what someone found with what they wanted to do next.

### Decision 02 — Let People Express Their Intent Naturally.

A trip might begin with a destination, a budget, a collection of places, or a few free days. A fixed set of search fields can be difficult to adapt to those different starting points. I designed a conversational planner so people could describe what they wanted in their own words and bring in existing context, including images, PDFs, notes, and saved lists. This gives them one place to bring their information together without having to organize it all first.

### Decision 03 — Turn Generated Answers Into Usable Interfaces.

Conversation is useful for describing a trip, but a long response containing places, times, and recommendations can be difficult to review. I designed the experience to move from conversational input into structured trip views, where people can inspect options, open an itinerary, and review activities day by day. Conversation helps people express their intent; the schedule helps them make decisions. The generated response becomes the starting point for working with a plan.

## 04 / Designing the System

### Reduce Complexity Through Consistency.

Spots includes different types of content and interactions, so the challenge was to support its features without making the interface difficult to understand. I explored variations of the home experience, discovery layouts, navigation, contextual actions, and planning interfaces. Shared navigation and recurring interaction patterns help people move between activities, while more specific controls appear where they are needed.

A place can be inspected in a detail view, a collection can provide context for planning, and a conversation can lead to an itinerary. The interface changes to support each activity while keeping familiar patterns across the product.

## 05 / The Product

### Discover. Plan. Go.

Spots brings recommendations, saved collections, conversational planning, and itineraries into one experience. People can discover places, use those places and their travel preferences to build a plan, and review the result through a structured schedule. The journey moves from finding somewhere interesting to understanding how it fits into a trip.

## 06 / Reflection

### Design The Connections, Not Just The Screens.

Working as the sole designer within a small startup team means thinking about Spots as a complete system. Discovery, lists, profiles, conversations, and itineraries each have their own design challenges; the larger task is deciding how they should connect. The work has taught me to pay closer attention to those transitions: what information carries forward, which controls stay familiar, and when the interface needs to change.

My work on Spots is ongoing. One area I would like to evaluate further is whether people understand how to take a place they discover and include it in a trip. The underlying idea is to make the distance between finding a place and going there smaller.
